# Injury and Recovery

## Goal

Injury risk creates workload tension and career stories without becoming random punishment or medical micromanagement.

## Inputs

- Body/readiness;
- Durability;
- workload/snap volume;
- recent training intensity;
- existing injury state;
- position exposure;
- recovery actions/cards;
- deterministic RNG.

## Injury representation

Use fictionalized, non-diagnostic gameplay categories such as:

- minor knock/restriction;
- short absence;
- multi-week injury;
- major season-impacting injury.

Specific names may be used for flavor only when medically appropriate and not implying medical advice.

## Player agency

When useful, offer choices such as:

- rest/rehab and miss development/opportunity;
- play limited with reduced readiness;
- return aggressively with increased re-aggravation risk.

Do not make the optimal answer obvious every time.

## Recovery

Recovery is influenced by Body actions, time, cards, facilities, and injury state. High-end program facilities may improve recovery/development but should not eliminate injury risk.

## Fairness

- communicate major risk factors;
- avoid catastrophic injury from a single invisible arbitrary roll when no prior risk exists unless extremely rare and intentionally designed;
- preserve seeds for reproduction;
- simulation should reveal whether particular actions/positions create unrealistic injury rates.

## M7 four-position lifecycle contract

The staged lifecycle projects injury exposure for QB, RB, WR, and CB from the same legible Body, Durability, projected workload, and recent-training inputs plus a position-owned exposure value. The position value is explicit saved-design evidence, not a hidden branch or a renamed WR risk. A current restriction continues to own availability and opportunity cap; the projection does not diagnose an injury, roll a new outcome, or fabricate participation. Live assessment remains deferred until the public four-position command composes this projection with the established deterministic injury outcome catalog.

The shared browserless catalog sampler now serves the established WR path: canonical eligible IDs, one risk draw, and a second weighted-outcome draw only on a hit. Ongoing restrictions do not sample another injury. Rest/limited choice arithmetic and duration recovery are shared zero-draw functions; the owning weekly command must enforce assessment/choice/rollover exactly once. Historical WR evidence stays literal. Added-position assessment must retain its own explicitly tagged exposure evidence rather than fabricate the different WR component shape.

The staged `position_injury_week_v1` assessment now composes that position projection and shared catalog. It retains bounded source inputs, exact exposure, outcome/rolls, current injury, and before/after RNG. A limited restriction has null availability until the player chooses; no-injury and out outcomes have explicit neutral availability. Reload validation replays the same assessment and optional choice, rejecting forged caps, state effects, catalog mismatches, or draw evidence. This function alone does not publish career history or advance a week; the owning saved command must bind the source to the actual prepared athlete and carry restrictions/recovery exactly once.

Staged v2 regular-season commands now own that boundary: assessment follows practice review, an unresolved limited choice persists `INJURY_CHOICE`, and preview starts only with explicit availability. Current histories retain the actual assessment; next-week current injury is the prior settled injury after one duration decrement plus its saved recovery credit. Repeated reads/reviews cannot recover twice or reroll. This is not final browser activation: relationship/event/academic/NIL handling must precede this boundary, the three-drill-only added-position catalog must gain meaningful recovery and off-field options with injury-aware availability, and postseason/season transitions must carry the correct injury history.
