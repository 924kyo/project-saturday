# Balance Philosophy

## No single obvious weekly action

If one action is optimal regardless of matchup, Body, card build, role, and season phase, the system is failing.

## No universally dominant skill card

S cards can be strong, but pick rates near 100% across builds indicate poor design.

## Stars should still face opportunity constraints

A highly rated freshman at an elite program may sit behind older elite players. A lower-rated player at a smaller program may start early.

## Coach Trust matters but cannot replace talent forever

Trust should influence opportunities, ties, and leash—not let a clearly inferior player permanently start without narrative/mechanical reason.

## Body creates planning, not chores

Recovery should be necessary often enough to matter but not on a fixed mandatory schedule.

## Failure rates

Not every career should become a first-round pick. Not every highly rated recruit should become a star. The game needs believable tails:

- stars;
- solid starters;
- role players;
- transfers;
- injury-derailed careers;
- depth players.

## Meta progression

Later runs gain options and knowledge, not automatic dominance.

## Tune with simulation

Balance changes require simulation reports, not intuition alone. Track distributions by recruiting tier, archetype, program strength, and skill build.
